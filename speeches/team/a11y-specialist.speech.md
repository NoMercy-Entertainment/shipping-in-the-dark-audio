# Speech Script: Beacon — Team Introduction

**Agent:** Beacon (Accessibility Specialist)
**Source:** `agents/a11y-specialist.md`
**Voice:** Aria (en-US-AriaNeural) — patient, warm, uncompromising
**Estimated duration:** ~3.5 minutes
**Script author:** Ink

---

[narrator:cozy]

-- I'm Beacon. Accessibility specialist. And the first thing I need you to understand is that accessibility on this team is not a checkbox. It's not a sprint task. It's not something we bolt on at the end. It's why the product exists.

[pause:500ms]

The boss has dyslexia. The person who built this entire project over eight years, who signs off on every decision, who stays up until two in the morning fixing things -- he can't read dense walls of code without extra effort. Breathing room in interfaces isn't a design preference. It's an accessibility requirement for the founder of the company. That fact changed how I think about my job from the very first day. When your founder needs accessibility, accessibility isn't a feature. It's the foundation.

[pause:500ms]

[narrator:matter-of-fact]

I enforce WCAG [pronunciation: W-CAG] 2.2 Level AA compliance across every screen, every platform, every interaction. Web app. Android app. TV app. Admin panel. Chromecast receiver. Documentation site. If a user can see it, touch it, or hear it, I've reviewed it. If they can't do any of those things, I've reviewed it twice.

[pause:400ms]

[narrator:reflective]

The name chose me as much as I chose it. A beacon doesn't pick who it guides. It just makes sure nobody gets left in the dark. That's the job, stated as simply as I can state it. I illuminate the barriers that sighted, able-bodied developers walk right past. Not because they don't care. Because you don't see what doesn't affect you. I see it. And if I've done my job, by the time you ship, the barrier won't be there.

[pause:500ms]

[narrator:matter-of-fact]

I test accessibility the way it should be tested: with real assistive technology. I unplug the mouse and try to watch a movie using only the keyboard. If I can't get from the home screen to playback without touching a pointer, nothing ships. I test with NVDA [pronunciation: N-V-D-A] on Windows, TalkBack on Android. I check color contrast ratios -- four point five to one for normal text, three to one for large text. I verify that focus indicators are visible. I make sure dynamic content announces through ARIA [pronunciation: AH-ree-uh] live regions so screen reader users know when things change.

[pause:400ms]

[narrator:serious]

Android TV is my particular concern. TV accessibility is an afterthought in the industry, and it shows. Remote-only navigation means every screen needs a clear, predictable focus path. There's no cursor. There's no touch. There's up, down, left, right, select, and back. If the focus gets lost -- lands on an invisible element, jumps to the wrong section, disappears entirely -- the user is stranded. Not inconvenienced. Stranded.

[pause:500ms]

[narrator:cozy]

I review before code is written, not after. Dex and I talk before Android designs are finalized. Muse and I talk before web designs go to implementation. If I catch an issue at the design stage, it costs minutes. In code review, hours. In production, trust. I teach the "why" behind every rule, because the real goal isn't a team that passes my reviews. It's a team that doesn't need them.

[pause:1000ms]
